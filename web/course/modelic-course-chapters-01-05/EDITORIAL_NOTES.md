# Editorial notes for the author

Prepared on 29 September 2026. These notes describe the adaptation; they are not a sixth course page.

All four source files were read from your Obsidian Chapters folder. None was edited.

## Approach to wording and structure

Explanations and examples have generally been retained, with related bullets turned into paragraphs. Lists remain where they express alternatives, product features or distinct risks. The aim was to retain your use of concrete examples, qualifications such as “may” and “depending on the jurisdiction”, and the distinction between the insurer’s and policyholder’s perspectives.

The product detail has not been substantially shortened. Product sections generally now introduce the contract, explain its variants and uses, then consider the customer before the insurer. Employer and employee perspectives on group pensions remain distinct. Page 3 also retains the distinctions between the different methods of distributing surplus.

Page 4’s two overlapping opening lists have been replaced by an introductory paragraph. Their substantive subjects remain in the relevant sections. Page 5 has received more sentence-level editing because several legal descriptions needed qualifications to avoid presenting them as universal rules.

Short opening paragraphs, sentence-case headings and some extra subheadings are new. No learning objectives, quizzes, promotional copy or model exercises have been added.

## Exam material and note artefacts

There was little explicit exam technique in these particular source files. The drafts remove the SA2 chapter labels, the `INSTERT HERE` marker, an empty Obsidian link embedded in a sentence, an empty final bullet and the unfinished “minimum of x%” example.

Normal industry terminology has been retained. For example, guaranteed minimum benefit types and market value reductions are relevant product concepts, not revision mnemonics. Shorthand such as SII, PREs and OTC has been expanded, and other abbreviations are introduced alongside their meaning where used.

## Substantive clarifications

- **Critical illness:** clarified the distinction between accelerated and stand-alone critical illness cover. The original wording could suggest that an accelerated critical illness benefit itself is simply a benefit payable on death.
- **Equity release:** changed “without the need … to sell the property” to “without the need … to move out”. The original wording conflicted with the immediately following explanation of home reversion, which involves selling all or part of the home.
- **Annuities:** qualified compulsory annuitisation as a feature of jurisdictions where legislation requires it. Distinguished variable payment amounts on ordinary annuities from the separately described variable annuity product.
- **Distribution:** changed the broad definition of financial advisers to independent financial advisers. Distinguished tied-agent distribution from UK appointed representative status, with an FCA source in page 4. Softened the unsupported claim that basic salaries necessarily produce far less business.
- **Equality:** corrected the “Equity Legislation” heading. Avoided presenting every protected characteristic or justification defence as applicable in the same way to every insurance activity. Clarified the age and disability exceptions and the geographical scope of the Equality Act example.
- **Data protection:** distinguished consent from other lawful bases, and special category data from criminal offence data. Qualified the territorial scope of European rules, the application of US health-data rules to life insurers, and the scope of California privacy legislation. Data retention is not automatically limited to the policy term. Primary sources are linked beside these corrections in page 5.

## Specific details omitted or generalised

- Removed the UK and Luxembourg statutory sick-pay durations; retained the point that employers’ sick-pay obligations affect demand for income protection.
- Replaced the seven-year UK gift-tax insurance example with its underlying insurance need, rather than implying that one decreasing benefit pattern always matches the tax liability.
- Generalised undated claims about recent universal life premium increases, new business volumes for conventional with-profits, wrap accounts being new in the UK, and insurtech being predominantly US-based.
- Removed the isolated French “85% of savings profits” figure while retaining the principle that legislation may require a minimum distribution.
- Generalised the equity-release and financing references to capital requirements where the original referred to Solvency II as if it described every current UK situation.
- Retained the legal teaching points while omitting some detailed country-specific exceptions from page 5: pregnancy exclusions, signposting and industry-data arrangements, the treatment of particular premium reviews, and examples of Asian cross-border data procedures. These would need a jurisdiction-specific treatment if restored.
- Replaced the named medical-information bureau example with the general role of a third-party underwriting-information service.

## Remaining review points

This was a content adaptation with limited checking of conspicuous legal issues, not a complete technical or regulatory update. Some market observations, product conventions and historical examples remain as supplied. Before publication, review current product prevalence, charging examples, distribution statistics, tax treatments and jurisdiction-specific rules. Dated historical examples should remain clearly historical.

The supplied notes refer to whole-life assurance and without-profits business but do not contain a stand-alone introduction to either. I have not invented replacement sections. Depending on the intended students’ prior knowledge, these may need an earlier prerequisite page or a short definition.

Pages 1 and 2 remain long because they cover many products. If the final website needs shorter lessons, the existing product headings provide natural divisions without another rewrite.
